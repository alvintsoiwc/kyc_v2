# Source-of-wealth guidance notes (prototype corpus)

version: 2026-01-prototype
source: Paraphrased study notes prepared by the project team from the HKMA Guideline on AML/CFT (For Authorized Institutions), May 2023. NOT the official text; verify against the current official guideline before any use.

## 1. Source of wealth vs source of funds
Source of wealth describes how a customer accumulated their overall wealth (for example employment income, sale of property, business activity, inheritance). Source of funds describes the origin of the particular funds or assets involved in a specific transaction or relationship. A review of source of wealth looks at the activities that generated the customer's total net worth, not only the latest transfer.

## 2. Risk-based approach to corroboration
The extent of verification should be proportionate to the assessed risk of the relationship. For higher-risk customers, institutions are expected to take reasonable measures to establish source of wealth and source of funds, which can include corroborating the customer's declaration with documents or reliable independent information.

## 3. Employment income
Typical corroboration of employment income includes recent payslips, an employment letter stating position, start date and salary, salaries tax assessments or returns, and bank statements showing salary credits from the named employer. Checks should confirm that the employer exists and was operating during the stated employment period.

## 4. Property purchase and sale
Typical corroboration of a property transaction includes the sale and purchase agreement or assignment, a land search showing registered ownership and consideration, mortgage documents showing the loan amount, and bank records showing how the deposit and balance were paid or where sale proceeds were received.

## 5. Funding gaps and unexplained wealth
Where the documented sources do not account for the wealth or the payment, the gap should be identified and the customer asked to explain and evidence it. An unexplained gap is a reason for further enquiry, not by itself evidence of wrongdoing.

## 6. Third-party funding and gifts
Where funds come from a third party (for example a family gift or loan), institutions commonly seek a signed gift or loan letter, evidence of the transfer, and some understanding of the third party's own source of wealth.

## 7. Documentation of the review
The review should be documented, recording what was declared, what evidence was obtained, inconsistencies found and how they were resolved, and outstanding questions. Decisions to establish or continue a relationship are taken by authorised staff, not by automated tools.

## 8. Ongoing monitoring and returning customers
Information obtained in earlier reviews can be relied on where it remains up to date and relevant; material changes, new transactions or new sources of wealth should be examined.
